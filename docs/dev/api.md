# The HTTP API

The cabinet calls the API with `Authorization: Bearer <token>` (issued at
sign-in with a code). Another business answers `404`, owner-only actions
`403`, validation errors `422`, every error as `{"error": ..., "message":
...}`. The full description is `/docs` and `/openapi.json` (committed as
`web/openapi.json`, from which the cabinet's typed client is generated:
`just gen`). Versioning, deprecation and the public API's frozen contract are
in [`docs/api-versioning.md`](../api-versioning.md); every change goes to
[`docs/API_CHANGELOG.md`](../API_CHANGELOG.md).
`tests/platform/test_operator_guides.py` checks that the table below names
every route of the description and nothing that does not exist.

## Autotests and going live

Autotests never run inside a request: `POST …/assistant-versions` and
`POST …/assistant-versions/{id}/autotests` start a run (the version is
`testing`, the run `running`, `202` for a repeated run), and the background
worker plays it. `GET …/assistant-versions/{id}/autotest-run` shows the
progress: while the run is `running`, `scenario_count` is the number of
planned scenarios and `results` those already played (the worker stores them
after each scenario). Only a run over every language and scenario of the
version gives the status `ready`. A version may go live with a running trial
or a paid subscription, an accepted DPA, a manager contact and no blocking
gaps in the profile. The free trial starts at the first go-live, not when
the business is created: when there was none yet, the first publication
opens it on the business's plan (monthly) and records the milestone
`went_live`; a trial started earlier is not restarted.

The launch checklist is `GET …/assistant-versions/{id}/go-live-readiness`:
the items `subscription_or_trial` (`trial_at_go_live`: the trial starts at
launch), `dpa`, `profile_gaps` (the kinds of gaps), `staff_contact`,
`autotests` (the version's and the run's status) and, for versions with
voice, `voice_configuration`, each with `is_ok`, `is_blocking` and
`details`. The same codes come in refusals of a publication or rollback: the
error body may hold `"reasons": [{"code", "message", "details"}]` (also
`version_already_live`, `version_archived`, `version_not_archived`,
`force_publish_admin_only`). An unreadable menu link gets `422` with the
reason `menu_link_invalid`, `menu_link_unreachable` or
`menu_link_unreadable`; an unavailable model `502`. A body without reasons
stays the plain `{"error", "message"}`.

The answer of `POST …/test-chat` names the version that answered
(`assistant_version_id`, `assistant_version_number`) and the turn's tool
calls (`tool_calls`); without `assistant_version_id` the newest version that
is not archived answers. When it lacks the owner's latest edits (or there is
no version at all), a draft is built from the current profile for the owner
first, so "Try the assistant" works before launch and always with the latest
changes; the draft publishes nothing and is not checked. The lists
`GET …/knowledge` and `GET …/unanswered-questions` are paged: `?limit=&cursor=`,
the answer `{"items", "next_cursor"}`, filters applied before paging. A menu
import returns a `batch_id`; `DELETE …/knowledge/import/{batch_id}` deletes
that import's unconfirmed drafts.

## Importing from a website

`POST …/knowledge/import-website {"url"}` (`202`) queues a worker job
(`import_website`, the default lane) that reads `sitemap.xml` or the start
page and up to 15 pages of the same site, one model call per page; what it
finds becomes drafts (disabled) like a menu import's, with the source page's
address (`source_page_url`), confirmed or deleted with the same
`…/knowledge/import/confirm` and `DELETE …/knowledge/import/{batch_id}`. The
progress is in `GET …/knowledge/import-website/current` and in the events
`knowledge_import.progress`. A business runs one import at a time
(`409 website_import_running`) and at most 10 an hour (`429`); the model
calls' cost is recorded. `SafeHttpFetcher` (`app/clients/http/`) downloads
the pages: only `http`/`https` on ports 80 and 443, no credentials in the
URL; the address is resolved once and every IP checked (private, loopback,
link-local, CGNAT, cloud metadata, special IPv6 and IPv4-in-IPv6 addresses
are refused), and the connection goes to exactly the checked IP, again after
each of at most three redirects; at most 5 MB (after decompression) and 10 s
per page, only HTML, text and XML. The page's text is cleaned of scripts,
styles and hidden text and passed to the model as untrusted data. A menu link
is read by the same client. A private address is refused at once: `422` with
the reason `website_link_invalid`.

## Guided launch and Apply changes

The "Create an AI assistant" button is `POST /v1/assistants`: a business with
the defaults of its creator's country (time zone, languages, currency), its
launch steps and the niche's ready answers. The ready answers
(`app/registries/niches/starters/`) exist for all 16 niches in English,
Russian and Georgian; the hours follow the country's working week (weekends
from CLDR: Friday and Saturday in Israel). They are suggestions, not the
business's facts: they reach the profile only through
`POST …/setup/starter-answers/apply`, fill only empty sections and hold no
prices, so the "no prices" gap stays until the owner writes them.

`GET …/setup` derives the steps from the business's data (profile gaps, a
staff contact, connected channels, a tried assistant, a published version),
so nothing is ticked by hand; only skipped optional steps and milestones are
stored (`went_live`, `test_chat_tried`, `first_conversation`,
`first_booking`, `first_handoff`, each with the time it happened and
`celebrated_at`). The first real conversation, booking and handoff are found
from the business's earliest records (the sandbox of the test chat and
autotests does not count). The answer carries "check from your phone" links:
the website chat's page (`/widget/demo?business_id=…`, when the API is
reachable over HTTPS and the website chat is on) and the business's Telegram
bot; `is_answering` says whether they answer yet (after launch).

After launch the `guide` of the same answer leads to the first customers:
"Check from your phone" (the chat page's QR; the step counts when the first
inbound message arrives from the owner's number or contact — from WhatsApp
or Telegram, or in the page's chat while a `phone-check` runs), "Connect a
second channel" and "Show customers where to write" (a printed card, a
downloaded QR or the first visit to the chat page). The milestone "first
booking after hours" joins the others; the first real conversation, the
first booking and the first after-hours booking reach the owner as a
notification (Telegram, e-mail, devices) and in the cabinet as a
congratulation, once. The background jobs `notice_milestones` (every 10
minutes) and `send_activation_nudges` (hourly) notice milestones without an
open cabinet and send setup reminders: on days 1, 3 and 7 without a launch,
on days 2 and 5 after launch without a second channel or a first
conversation, on day 10 without a printed QR or visits to the chat page;
each once per business (`nudges_sent`), between 10:00 and 19:00 of the
business's time, with a link straight to the right screen; they are turned
off in Settings → Notifications.

`POST …/assistant/apply` builds a version from the current profile and
knowledge, checks the launch conditions other than autotests at once (a
staff contact, the agreement, payment, the profile) and hands the autotests
to the background worker; when they pass, the worker publishes the version.
Pressing again while an application runs, or when everything live is
current, starts nothing. A version that was checked and is not outdated is
published at once, without new autotests. `GET …/assistant/apply` shows the
stages (`checks_done` of `checks_total` while checks run); `needs_attention`
lists the reasons (`profile_incomplete`, `staff_contact_missing`,
`agreement_not_accepted`, `payment_needed`, `build_failed`, `checks_failed`
with the scenario kinds, `checks_stopped`, `voice_not_ready`,
`publish_failed`) with a text in the owner's language and the place to fix
it. The start of an application and the publication are audited. Versions,
autotests and manual publishing stay in the Advanced section.

`GET …/assistant/pending-changes` lists what the owner changed since the
live version: the assistant is built again from the current data (nothing is
stored) and its fact table is compared with the live version's row by row.
The changes are typed (`area`, `action` and the field, weekday, link kind,
special day's date or the owner's item name); a new price has the old and the
new one (`detail: price`, `before`, `after`). A special day in the past is not
a change. Tone, restrictions and handoff rules appear as one change
`conversation`, calls by plan as `calls`. The same computation answers
`has_unapplied_changes` in `GET …/assistant/apply`. Owner checks the live
version has not passed yet (new or changed after its run, unless "Check now"
passed on it) come as a separate list `owner_checks` and count in `count`:
the next Apply changes asks them first. `drafts` are versions built after the
live one that never reached customers; the owner may throw a draft away. When
an update fails an owner check, `checks_failed` names it (`failed_checks`:
the question, what the answer should have done, the reason in the owner's
language and the test answer, which can be fixed).

After the first launch Apply changes does not check every scenario in every
language but runs a quick check of the changes: the scenarios the changes
touch and three main ones in the main language (the main ones in every
language when the languages changed), plus a price question for each changed
item. The rules stay: every scenario with prices and bookings passes and the
average score is at least 4. The first launch and a manual run from Advanced
play every scenario. Drafts older than the version that went live leave the
version list (`discarded_at`) but can still be read by link. The test
bookings of one test conversation take no time in others.

With `LLM_PROVIDER=scripted` (development, staging, the cabinet's e2e) the
model plays a rehearsal: the judge gives every criterion 5, the AI customer
writes one sentence for its goal in the scenario's language, the assistant
answers in the customer's language, books the first free time of the next
days and hands the conversation to a person when asked for one or when
something is urgent. So Apply changes reaches live without keys; such a
model does not measure answer quality (the replayed evals do, see
[ADR 0008](../adr/0008-offline-replay-evals.md)).

## The shared inbox

Owners and staff see conversations in five views: "needs a person" (handed
to a person), "requests" (a new lead or one in progress), "mine" and
"unassigned" (among those waiting for the team: a person needed or a lead
open) and "all". The views are pages over the indexes of migration 1053, the
counters one grouped count of waiting conversations. Any staff member sees a
row: the customer's name and phone, the channel, the start of the last
message, the assignee, an open handoff and lead without free text, the number
of notes; no model cost, tool calls or note texts.

Assignment is a compare-and-set on `assignment_revision`: of two
simultaneous clicks one wins and the other gets 409 `assignment_changed`
(refresh the conversation and repeat). A staff member assigns a free
conversation or their own to themselves, a colleague or nobody; only an
owner reassigns a colleague's conversation (otherwise 403
`assigned_to_colleague`), and only a member of the business can be assigned
(422 `not_a_member`). Saving a conversation turn never undoes an assignment.
Auto-assignment, when on (`PUT …/inbox/settings`), gives a new handoff or
lead to the chosen members (or every staff member, or the owners), to the one
with the fewest waiting conversations, round robin on a tie; an
auto-assignment error never gets in the way of the handoff itself. Changes go
to the live events (`conversation.assigned`, `conversation.note`) and the
audit log.

The team's notes are stored apart from messages: they reach neither the
model's request, nor the customer, nor staff notifications; creating and
deleting them is audited. A note's author or an owner may delete it. Notes
are part of a customer's data export and are deleted with their data.
