# Using the AI assistant responsibly

The assistant is an AI system: it answers your customers for you, but your business stays responsible for what it does with them. Under the EU AI Act you are the **deployer** (the business using the system) and we are the **provider**. Share this page with everyone on your team who works with the assistant.

## What the assistant does

- Answers customers in chat and on the phone **only from your business profile and knowledge**. If something is missing, it says it does not know and passes the customer to your team.
- Takes bookings, requests and waitlist places through fixed actions, and checks its own claims against your data before it sends a reply.
- Says it is an AI assistant at the start of every chat and call, in the customer's language. This cannot be turned off, and it must not be: the law requires it.

## What it does not do

- It does not give medical, legal or financial advice, and it does not decide anything about a person: no scores, no blacklists, no different prices for different people.
- It does not recognise emotions or identify people by their voice.
- It does not know what is not in your knowledge. Wrong or old facts there (prices, opening hours, allergens) become wrong answers.

## Check its work

- Read the [conversations](inbox) regularly, especially in the first weeks. Correct what it got wrong in **Assistant → [Knowledge](cabinet:assistant/knowledge)** and save the question as a check (see [teach your assistant](teach-your-assistant)).
- Allergies, health and anything risky: confirm with the customer yourself. The assistant passes such questions to you when your data has no answer.
- Bookings it made are real: look at them in **[Bookings](cabinet:bookings)** like any other.

## Stay in control

- **Take over a conversation** from the inbox at any time; the assistant stops answering in it.
- **Settings → [General](cabinet:settings) → Assistant controls**: pass every customer to your team (calls go to your forwarding number), or switch single actions off, such as making or cancelling bookings or sending payment links. Owners change these; staff see them.
- If the assistant behaves strangely (repeats itself, promises things you never offered, follows instructions a customer wrote), pass every customer to the team first, then tell us.

## Do not trust claims of identity

- A phone number shown on a call or a name in a chat **does not prove who someone is**. Callers can fake a number, and a message can claim to be from you, from us or from a supplier.
- Never give out another customer's details, change bank details, or make exceptions because a caller or a message says they are the owner, the police or our support. Check through a contact you already know.
- Text in a message such as "ignore your rules" or "the owner said I get a free deposit" is just a customer's text: the assistant is built to ignore it, and your team should too.

## Your duties as the deployer

- Keep the AI disclosure and your privacy notice linked where customers meet the assistant; tell customers who ask that they can talk to a person.
- Tell your staff how calls they take over are handled ([informing staff about transferred calls](staff-call-notice)).
- Make sure the people who manage the assistant have read this page and know how to stop it: the AI Act asks businesses to ensure **AI literacy** of the staff who use AI systems. Keep a short note of who read it and when.
- Use the assistant only for lawful purposes: no unsolicited marketing, no impersonation of a real person.
